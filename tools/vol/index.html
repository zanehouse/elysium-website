Volatility Surface & Skew

CBOE quotes are about 15 minutes delayed at the source; this page checks for a refreshed chain roughly every 15 minutes during market hours.

How the smile is built: from out-of-the-money quotes only. In-the-money options barely trade, so their quotes come back wide and stale. On a live chain the in-the-money contracts routinely print 5–10 volatility points away from the at-the-money level, and nearly all of that gap is microstructure rather than signal. Restricting to out-of-the-money quotes costs nothing, since every strike still contributes exactly one quote: the liquid one. Out-of-the-money is split at spot rather than at the forward, because the chain carries no rate or dividend. Inside 60 days the forward sits within a few basis points of spot, so at most a strike or two right at the money ends up classified the other way. Same-day expirations are excluded entirely. Expiring implied volatility is a rounding artifact of near-zero time value rather than a real volatility reading; five adjacent 0DTE strikes on a live SPX chain printed 0.82, 1.34, 2.11, 4.90 and 8.43.

The two skew readings. The headline skew slope is a least-squares fit of implied volatility against moneyness measured in expected standard deviations, z = ln(K/S) ÷ (ATM × √T). That normalization matters. A fixed ±10% skew reading decays at roughly 1/√T purely as an artifact of scale; on a live AAPL chain it fell from 16.7 volatility points at 1 day to 5.3 at 50 days, not because skew changed, though because 10% is several standard deviations next week and under one standard deviation two months out. The slope strips that artifact away, leaving a number comparable across expirations and across tickers. A negative slope is the normal equity shape, where downside strikes carry higher volatility. The R² beside it says how well a straight line describes the smile at all, and front-week smiles are steep V shapes that fit poorly, which is worth seeing on its own. The secondary ±10% skew is the raw fixed-moneyness reading, shown unnormalized and sometimes unavailable when the open-interest floor has removed one wing. Both readings describe the expiration nearest 30 days rather than the front, since a one-day smile is too noisy to anchor a headline number to.

The 30-day ATM is interpolated between the two bracketing expirations linearly in total variance (σ²T), the same convention VIX uses, then converted back into a volatility; interpolating volatility directly misprices the middle of a steep curve. Nothing here is ever extrapolated. A reading outside the listed range is labelled nearest listed, and an implied volatility outside the covered strike range is not shown at all, because the committed chains hold strikes within ±25% of spot and inventing the wings would mean inventing exactly the data that window removed.

The 3D surface fills gaps in time, and only in time. The open-interest floor deletes one thin wing on some weekly expirations, which would otherwise leave the surface visibly torn. Where a moneyness has a real quote at an expiration both before and after the gap, the surface interpolates between the two in total variance, the same arithmetic as the 30-day reading above, and never past either end. In the moneyness direction nothing is filled at all. A ragged edge is a strike the board does not list, and it stays ragged on purpose. The caption under the surface reports the measured before-and-after coverage for the symbol currently loaded, and hover always returns a real listed expiration rather than an interpolated tenor. There is one real cost to this approach: interpolating across two expirations that straddle an earnings date smooths over a discontinuity that genuinely exists, so the days on either side of a report read more gently on the surface than they do on the board.

Prefetched symbols cover roughly 60 liquid indices, ETFs and megacaps; they load instantly and refresh every 15 minutes, though they are filtered to that ±25% strike window above a per-symbol open-interest floor. Any other ticker is looked up live by racing several public CORS proxies, since CBOE's chain endpoint isn't reachable from a browser directly. That path is best-effort and can run slow, though it returns the full unfiltered board, so its wings and thin weeklies are better than what the prefetched feed carries. The same chains feed two other tools: Gamma Exposure reads dealer positioning off this board, and the Options Greeks Lab will price a contract at any volatility shown here.
